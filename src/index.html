Pacific Dataviz Challenge · 2026

The Pacific’s transition is not one story.

Two speeds sit side by side: clean-energy uptake and the emissions burden still carried by each system. Across seven island states, the useful question is not who is “winning”; it is where the starting points and policy choices differ.

What the snapshot says

Solomon Islands has the highest renewable share in this 2022 comparison.

50.08% of final energy consumption is renewable, while Tuvalu records the lowest emissions at 0.4 tonnes per person. These are descriptive contrasts, not a policy ranking.

A

Low emissions ≠ low vulnerability

Per-capita emissions show contribution to the problem, not exposure to its impacts.

B

Renewables ≠ one technology

The official indicator is a share of final energy consumption, not installed capacity.

Keep the caveat visible

Evidence is strongest when its edges are clear.

This explorer is a comparison tool, not a causal model. It gives a shared vocabulary for the next policy question.

01

What we can see

Country-level differences in three official indicators, and how those signals change over time.

02

What we cannot see

Exposure to hazards, distribution within a country, energy reliability, prices, or the causes of a year-to-year change.

03

What to ask next

Which investments make clean energy dependable and affordable without shifting costs onto already exposed communities?
